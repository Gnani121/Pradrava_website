Fluids Design Directions

Four ways the site could look, none of them built on tiles. Each one is shown with your real content — the same headline, the same seven capability areas, the same figures — so you are comparing the design and nothing else.

Why the tiles had to go. The current site puts almost everything into the same rounded card: capability areas, personas, deployment modes, standards, reminder engines. When every kind of information wears the same shape, nothing is ranked and the eye has nowhere to land. All four directions below replace the card with a structure that carries meaning — a numbered specification, a status ledger, a reading order, or a single continuous list.
A

Datasheet

The site as an engineering document. Hairline rules, monospace metadata, a numbered specification table. It borrows its authority from the certificates and test reports your customers already read — which is a claim your competitors cannot easily copy.

01

Cylinder Operations

The full asset lifecycle — receiving inspection, allocation, filling, refill vendors, service, rental and return — with a movement ledger behind every step, so any bottle's history is a record rather than a recollection.

02

Quality & Compliance

A real laboratory system, not a checklist. Certificates to ISO 6141, measurement uncertainty to 6142, calibration to 17025, and an audit trail no one can rewrite after the fact.

03

Finance, GST & Billing

Indian statutory compliance handled at the transaction rather than at month end. The tax split is computed twice, independently, and an invoice whose two figures disagree does not issue.

06

Notifications & SLA

Seventy-one rules over sixty-five stages give every hand-off an owner, a deadline and a named person it escalates to when the clock runs out. None of it is written in code.

D

Split Rail

Your existing colours and voice, with every box removed. A statement column that stays put while a single continuous list scrolls beside it, and the figures set on a divided rail instead of in four little cards. The safest of the four — it is a refinement of what you have, not a replacement.
